01

Cast partial denture fundamentals

A removable partial prosthesis designed around the remaining teeth and tissues.

Definition and purpose

A cast partial denture (CPD) is a removable partial denture with a cast metal framework that supports replacement teeth and is designed for a partially edentulous arch. It helps restore missing teeth, function and appearance while preserving the remaining oral structures when properly designed, fabricated and maintained.

ComponentPurpose
Major connectorUnites the components on one side of the arch with those on the other; its form must respect tissues and provide sufficient rigidity.
Minor connectorJoins parts such as rests, clasps or denture-base components to the major connector.
Rest and rest seatThe rest sits in a prepared rest seat and provides support against tissueward movement.
Direct retainerUsually a clasp assembly or attachment that helps resist removal along the path of placement.
Reciprocal / bracing elementHelps counter horizontal forces from the retentive component and contributes to stability.
Indirect retainerIn appropriate distal-extension designs, helps resist rotation of the denture base away from the tissues.
Denture base and artificial teethThe base supports replacement teeth and contacts the edentulous ridge; its design depends on the case.

Support

Resistance to movement towards teeth and/or tissues under load. Rests and suitable tissue-bearing areas contribute according to the design.

Retention

Resistance to movement away from the tissues along the path of removal. A retentive clasp engages an appropriate undercut.

Stability

Resistance to horizontal displacement and unwanted rotation during function.

Path of insertion

The planned direction in which the prosthesis is placed and removed. Surveying helps determine a suitable path.

Paper tipFor “components of a CPD,” list the parts and write one function beside each. For “support, retention and stability,” state the direction of the movement each resists.
02

Dental surveyor & surveying

A surveyor helps analyse the cast relative to a chosen path of insertion.

Definitions

A dental surveyor is a parallelometer used to determine and record the relative parallelism and contours of tooth and tissue surfaces on a cast. Surveying is the systematic examination of those surfaces to plan the prosthesis and its path of insertion.

Main parts and tools

Part / toolUse
Base, vertical column and horizontal armSupport the instrument and hold the surveying tool in a consistent orientation.
Adjustable cast tableHolds the cast and allows it to be tilted and secured in the selected orientation.
Analysing rodHelps evaluate parallelism, potential guiding planes and interferences.
Carbon markerMarks the height of contour, commonly called the survey line, relative to the selected cast position.
Undercut gaugesHelp locate an appropriate undercut depth for the prescribed retentive component.
Wax trimmer / bladeUsed in surveying procedures such as shaping blockout wax parallel to the selected path.

What does surveying identify?

  • A feasible path of insertion and removal.
  • Guiding planes and areas that may interfere with placement.
  • Tooth contours and usable retentive undercuts.
  • Areas requiring blockout and design modifications.
  • A method of recording the cast's selected orientation, commonly by tripoding or another reproducible reference method.
Inspect diagnostic cast→Place cast on surveyor→Analyse contours & tilt→Select path of insertion→Identify guiding planes, interferences & undercuts→Mark survey lines→Record cast position→Develop / communicate design
Survey line versus undercutThe survey line marks the height of contour for the selected orientation. The undercut is the area below that height relative to the chosen path; not every undercut is suitable for clasp retention. Retention depends on the prescribed design, tooth and clasp material.
03

CPD design & fabrication

Learn the clinical–laboratory sequence without confusing responsibility for design.

Design considerations

  1. Review the clinician's prescription, diagnostic cast, missing teeth and condition of abutments and tissues.
  2. Select an appropriate path of insertion and identify guiding planes and retention using the surveyor.
  3. Plan support, direct and indirect retention where required, stability and a suitably rigid major connector.
  4. Place components to respect gingival tissues, function, aesthetics and cleanability.
  5. Communicate findings and confirm the final design with the clinician before fabrication.

Conventional laboratory workflow

Receive prescription & casts→Survey & confirm design→Prepare master cast: blockout / relief→Duplicate cast as needed→Refractory cast & wax framework→Sprue, invest & cast→Divest, finish & inspect framework→Framework try-in by clinician→Teeth arrangement / try-in→Process base, finish & deliver

Blockout fills selected undesirable undercuts so the framework can be fabricated and removed along the selected path. Relief provides planned space where needed. Both are design-specific: do not block out the intended clasp undercut indiscriminately.

Digital workflow

Scan cast / import records→Digital survey & path selection→Mark features & design framework in CAD→Review design with clinician→Manufacture by validated process→Finish & verify on cast / clinically

Manufacture may involve a printed pattern followed by casting, or a validated direct manufacturing route such as selective laser melting, depending on the equipment and alloy. Digital design does not eliminate the need for support, retention, stability and fit checks.

04

Precision attachments & survey milling

Alternative retention and parallelism require careful planning.

Precision attachment

A precision attachment is a manufactured retentive connection with matching components, commonly used to connect a removable prosthesis to a fixed restoration or other supporting structure. Attachment systems differ; their indication and maintenance are case-specific.

  • Potential advantages include more discreet retention than visible clasps in selected cases.
  • Possible limitations include cost, space requirements, technique sensitivity, wear and future maintenance.
  • Component choice, position, insertion path, support and replacement requirements must be coordinated with the clinician and system instructions.

Survey milling

Survey milling uses a milling arrangement and the chosen insertion path to create or refine prescribed parallel surfaces on a restoration or framework. The aim is to produce compatible guiding or functional surfaces as part of the planned prosthesis—not to mill indiscriminately.

Confirm prescription & design→Establish common path→Position restoration / framework→Mill prescribed surfaces→Inspect alignment, fit & finish→Confirm with clinician
Do not overgeneralizeNot every CPD requires precision attachments or survey milling. Selection depends on the clinical case, space, support, maintenance and the chosen system.
05

Implant prosthetic foundations

Identify the components before learning the laboratory steps.

Core terms

TermExam-ready meaning
Dental implantA device placed in bone to support a dental prosthesis; commonly a threaded titanium or titanium-alloy fixture, depending on the system.
OsseointegrationA direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant.
AbutmentA component connecting an implant to, or supporting, a prosthesis.
Implant analogueA laboratory replica of the relevant implant or abutment connection, used in a working model.
Impression copingA component used in conventional impressions to transfer the implant or abutment position and orientation.
Scan bodyA component with a recognized geometry used to digitally capture the implant or abutment position and orientation.
Implant-retained prosthesisA prosthesis whose retention involves implants; it may be fixed or removable depending on the design.
Hybrid prosthesis / dentureOften refers to a fixed implant-supported full-arch prosthesis combining a framework with replacement teeth and gingival-coloured material; terminology varies.

Fixed versus removable and screw versus cement

Fixed versus removable describes how the prosthesis is removed: a fixed prosthesis is removed by the clinician; a removable overdenture is removed by the patient for hygiene. Screw-retained versus cement-retained describes how a fixed restoration is retained; these are a separate distinction.

Screw-retained fixed restoration

Retained by a prosthetic screw and generally retrievable by the clinician. Screw-access position and space must be considered in design.

Cement-retained fixed restoration

Retained by cement to an abutment. The clinical team considers retrievability, access and control of excess cement.

Critical compatibility pointImplant connections, scan bodies, analogues, abutments, screws and digital libraries are system-specific. Verify the exact implant platform and prescription; components are not automatically interchangeable. Use manufacturer torque instructions only in the clinical setting.
06

Implant laboratory workflows

Position transfer → model/design → fabricate → verify.

Conventional impression-based route

Clinician records implant position with impression components→Lab verifies prescription, implant system & impression→Connect compatible analogue as indicated→Fabricate working model / soft-tissue replica as needed→Design abutment / framework→Fabricate prosthesis→Inspect and send for clinical fit check

Digital route

Clinician scans correct scan body & oral records→Lab receives and checks files→Select matching system & digital library→Align scan body and design components→Manufacture model / prosthesis as indicated→Inspect fit, contacts & access→Clinical verification

What the technician checks

  • Correct patient, prescription, implant system, platform and components.
  • Complete records, verified scan-body alignment or sound impression transfer.
  • Fit of the framework and interface; no assumption that a digital design automatically fits.
  • Prosthetic contour, occlusion on available records, hygiene access and planned screw access or attachment position.
  • Communication of uncertainties or missing information before fabrication.

Passive fit describes a framework fit that seats on its supports without introducing undue strain from misfit. Assessment and verification involve clinical and laboratory steps; a single laboratory check does not guarantee clinical passivity.

Hybrid full-arch prosthesis

In a commonly used fixed hybrid design, a framework supports artificial teeth and gingival-coloured material over multiple implants. Key design concerns include accurate fit, occlusion, cleansability, phonetics, aesthetics, screw access and repair/maintenance. The clinical team plans implant position and the prosthetic design.

07

Complex case planning

The same logic connects CPDs, bridges, implants and combination cases.

Master flowchart: evaluate → plan → scan → design → process

Evaluate records, support & space→Plan function, aesthetics & hygiene with clinician→Scan / digitize records→Design framework & full contour→Review fit, path & materials→Manufacture / process→Finish & quality-check→Clinical try-in and adjustments

Questions to ask before design

  1. What is the prescribed prosthesis, which teeth or implants support it, and what is the planned retention?
  2. Are the records complete—casts/scans, opposing arch, occlusion and shade where relevant?
  3. Is there enough restorative space, and is the path of insertion suitable?
  4. Are connectors, attachment positions and hygiene spaces appropriate to the material and case?
  5. Will the design meet functional and aesthetic goals, and can it be maintained?
  6. Are system, material and manufacturing specifications confirmed?
Combination caseA case may combine fixed restorations, removable components and/or implant components. Coordinate the insertion path, support, retention and connections as one plan rather than designing each part in isolation.

09

Exam-ready short answers

For short questions, write a definition followed by the most relevant points.

1. Define a cast partial denture.

A cast partial denture is a removable partial prosthesis with a cast metal framework used to replace missing teeth in a partially edentulous arch. Its design provides appropriate support, retention and stability while respecting remaining teeth and tissues.

2. Differentiate support, retention and stability.

Support resists movement of a denture towards the teeth or tissues; retention resists removal away from the tissues; stability resists horizontal displacement and unwanted rotation.

3. What is a dental surveyor? State two uses.

A dental surveyor is a parallelometer used to analyse the relative contours and parallelism of surfaces on a cast. It helps determine a path of insertion, identify guiding planes and undercuts, and record the selected cast position.

4. What is a survey line?

A survey line marks the height of contour of a tooth or other surface relative to the chosen cast orientation and path of insertion. Undercuts are assessed in relation to that line.

5. What is blockout?

Blockout is the filling of selected undesirable undercuts on a cast to enable fabrication and removal of the framework along the planned insertion path. Intended retentive areas are handled according to the prescribed design.

6. State four components of a CPD and their functions.

A major connector unites components across the arch; rests provide support; direct retainers resist removal; and minor connectors join components to the major connector. Denture bases support artificial teeth.

7. Define a precision attachment.

A precision attachment is a manufactured retentive connection with matching components, often used between a removable prosthesis and a fixed restoration. It can provide discreet retention but requires space, accurate fabrication and maintenance.

8. What is survey milling?

Survey milling is the controlled production or refinement of prescribed parallel surfaces relative to a selected path of insertion. It helps create compatible guiding or functional surfaces for the planned prosthesis.

9. Differentiate an implant analogue and a scan body.

An implant analogue is a laboratory replica of the relevant implant or abutment connection used in a model. A scan body has a recognizable geometry that allows the implant or abutment position and orientation to be recorded digitally.

10. Define osseointegration.

Osseointegration is the direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant.

11. What is a hybrid implant prosthesis?

The term often describes a fixed implant-supported full-arch prosthesis in which a framework supports replacement teeth and gingival-coloured material. Accurate fit, occlusion, aesthetics, cleansability and maintenance are important.

12. Why must implant components be checked for compatibility?

Implant systems differ in their connections and components. Matching the prescribed platform, analogue, scan body, abutment, screw and digital library helps prevent inaccurate position transfer, poor fit or component damage.

13. Outline the digital implant laboratory workflow.

Receive verified scan records and prescription → select the correct system and library → align the scan body → design the prosthesis → manufacture as indicated → inspect fit, access and contacts → send for clinical verification.
