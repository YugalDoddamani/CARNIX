01

The CAD/CAM pathway

Digital acquisition, design, manufacture and verification are separate stages.

CAD means computer-aided design: digital planning and modelling of a restoration or device. CAM means computer-aided manufacturing: computer-controlled production of the design. A complete digital workflow also needs data capture, quality control and finishing.

Prescription & case records→Scan / acquire data→Inspect and prepare digital model→CAD design→CAM: mill / print / other validated method→Post-process & finish→Check fit & send for clinical verification
  • Check patient identification, prescription, implant platform where relevant, material and file completeness.
  • Design for the selected material, required clearance and manufacturing process.
  • A digital file does not guarantee a clinically acceptable fit: each stage adds potential error.
  • Protect patient data through approved access, transfer and backup procedures.
02

Additive versus subtractive

One builds the object; the other removes material from a blank.

FeatureAdditive manufacturingSubtractive manufacturing
PrincipleCreates a part by adding or consolidating material layer by layer.Cuts material away from a block or disc using tools.
ExamplesPhotopolymer resin printing; metal powder-bed fusion.Computer-controlled milling of compatible blanks.
StrengthsCan manufacture complex geometries and multiple objects in one build.Can use industrially produced blanks with controlled properties; widely used for restorations.
LimitationsLayer orientation, support removal and post-processing affect the result; available materials depend on system.Material waste, tool wear and limited access to fine internal geometry can affect production.
Final checksInspect build, remove supports where applicable, complete specified post-processing and assess fit.Inspect milling, complete material-specific processing and assess fit and finish.
Exam answerAdditive = layer-by-layer build; subtractive = removal from a larger blank. Compare by principle, example, advantage and limitation.
03

Milling & material handling

Material selection determines the tools, processing and finishing.

Review case & select compatible blank→Import approved design→Orient / nest & generate toolpaths→Fit blank and appropriate tools→Mill using approved settings→Remove and post-process→Inspect, finish & document
MaterialExample useHandling or follow-up
WaxPatterns for subsequent casting or pressing.Protect delicate pattern and margins; subsequent processing follows the pattern system.
PEEKSelected frameworks or prosthetic components where appropriate.Use compatible tools and manufacturer-approved joining and finishing procedures.
MetalFrameworks or components from compatible alloy blanks.Use the equipment and cooling / extraction method prescribed for the alloy; inspect fit and surface.
ZirconiaFrameworks or monolithic restorations.Many systems mill pre-sintered blanks with enlargement for later shrinkage; sinter and finish according to the specific system.

Dry versus wet milling: whether coolant is used depends on the machine and material. Do not assume a single method applies to every blank. Tool diameter and tool access limit some fine internal features; worn tools can affect surface and fit.

SafetyUse enclosed systems, appropriate dust extraction and protective equipment according to lab policy. Do not improvise with metal powder, resin or ceramic dust. Observe the equipment manufacturer's cleaning and maintenance procedures.
04

3D printing in dentistry

A digital model becomes a physical object through successive layers.

General resin-printing workflow

Prepare / verify design→Orient object & add supports→Slice into layers→Print with validated material & settings→Clean / remove supports as prescribed→Post-cure if required→Finish, inspect & document

In common resin systems, light cures a photopolymer resin. Build orientation, layer thickness, supports, cleaning and post-curing affect the result. A printed object is not ready for intended use until its prescribed post-processing and checks are complete.

Applications named in the syllabus

Surgical

Examples include validated surgical guides or anatomical models used in planning.

Orthodontic

Examples include dental models used in orthodontic workflows and selected approved appliances.

Prosthodontic

Examples include models, selected provisional components and patterns, depending on approved material indications.

Implantology

Examples include planning models, guides and compatible printed working-model components.

Important distinctionNot every printing resin is biocompatible or approved for every intraoral application. Use the intended-use indication, validation and post-processing specified for the exact material and device.
05

DMLS and metal powder-bed fusion

Digital design enables metal components to be built from powder.

DMLS (direct metal laser sintering) is a commercial term commonly used for a laser-based metal powder-bed fusion process. A laser selectively fuses regions of successive powder layers according to digital data. The exact terminology and degree of melting depend on the system and material.

Design & check framework→Orient and add supports→Build layer by layer from metal powder→Cool & safely remove part→Remove supports / post-process as specified→Finish and inspect fit

Applications can include compatible dental frameworks and copings. Potential benefits include production of complex shapes and multiple parts; limitations include equipment cost, specialist handling, surface finishing and quality assurance. Powder reuse, heat treatment and finishing are system-specific.

SafetyMetal powder handling can present inhalation and fire or explosion hazards. Only trained personnel should operate and service equipment using the manufacturer's procedures, controls and permitted extinguishing equipment.
06

Equipment & quality control

A reliable laboratory checks the process, not only the finished object.

Routine checks

  • Verify machine calibration, approved material, correct tools and current maintenance status.
  • Check file completeness, design orientation, margins and process parameters before manufacture.
  • Monitor tool wear, build defects, resin condition and equipment alarms as appropriate.
  • Inspect fit, margins, contacts, occlusion on available records, surface and cleanliness after processing.
  • Record the case, material batch, equipment cycle and any correction or remake according to lab policy.
Identify issue→Stop & document→Check records, material & equipment→Identify likely cause→Correct / remake if appropriate→Reinspect & communicate
07

Dental laboratory operations

A case should be traceable from receipt to delivery.

Receive prescription & case→Check identifiers, records & deadline→Decontaminate according to protocol→Plan & schedule→Fabricate & document→Quality check→Package, communicate & dispatch

Production management organizes work, staff, equipment and materials to meet quality and delivery requirements. Useful records include prescription details, material batches, work stages, checks, remake causes and delivery status. Confirm unclear instructions rather than guessing.

Human resource management

  • Assign roles and responsibilities according to competence.
  • Provide orientation, equipment training and continuing education.
  • Plan workload, supervision, communication and coverage.
  • Maintain safe working conditions and handle feedback and performance fairly.
08

Safety & infection control

Protect staff, prevent cross-contamination and document the process.

Core terminology

  • Cleaning: removal of visible soil and contamination; it generally precedes disinfection or sterilization.
  • Disinfection: a process that eliminates many or most pathogenic microorganisms on inanimate objects, but does not necessarily destroy all bacterial spores.
  • Sterilization: a validated process intended to destroy or remove all forms of microbial life, including spores.

General laboratory process

Receive item as potentially contaminated→Use appropriate PPE & prevent splashes→Clean and disinfect / sterilize as appropriate→Confirm compatibility & required contact time→Separate clean and contaminated areas→Communicate handling status

Use procedures compatible with the impression, cast, prosthesis or device and follow the clinic–laboratory agreement. Do not mix chemicals or use unverified concentrations. Sharps, contaminated waste, airborne dust, resins, hot equipment and electrical systems require appropriate controls.

Exam pointDisinfection is not the same as sterilization. The method, product and exposure time depend on the item, validated instructions and applicable local policy.
09

Ethics, jurisprudence & communication

Responsible work protects patients, colleagues and the laboratory.

Ethics concerns professional duties and responsible conduct; jurisprudence concerns the laws and legal principles governing practice. Exact legal duties differ by jurisdiction and professional role.

  • Work within training, scope, prescription and authorization; refer clinical decisions to the clinician.
  • Maintain confidentiality and use secure records and communication.
  • Record materials, processes and quality concerns honestly; never conceal defects.
  • Communicate missing information, delays, remakes and relevant safety concerns promptly.
  • Follow applicable regulations for infection control, equipment, waste and patient information.

Use your institution's current local rules and professional guidance for specific legal requirements; this guide does not state jurisdiction-specific law.

11

Exam-ready short answers

Definition first; then two or three relevant points or a flowchart.

1. Define CAD and CAM.

CAD is computer-aided design of an object using software. CAM is computer-aided manufacturing in which a computer-controlled system produces that design. Data capture and post-production quality checks are also needed in a complete workflow.

2. Differentiate additive and subtractive manufacturing.

Additive manufacturing builds a part layer by layer, for example by resin printing. Subtractive manufacturing removes material from a blank, for example by milling. Additive processes require build and post-processing checks; milling involves tool wear and material waste.

3. Outline a resin 3D-printing workflow.

Verify the design → orient and support → slice → print with the validated system → clean and remove supports as prescribed → post-cure if required → finish and inspect.

4. What is DMLS?

DMLS is a term used for a laser-based metal powder-bed fusion process in which selected areas of successive powder layers are fused according to a digital design. It can produce compatible metal frameworks but requires controlled handling, finishing and quality checks.

5. Name four materials that may be milled in dental workflows.

Examples include wax, PEEK, metal alloy and zirconia. Each requires compatible equipment, tools and product-specific post-processing.

6. Differentiate cleaning, disinfection and sterilization.

Cleaning removes soil; disinfection eliminates many or most pathogenic microorganisms on objects but does not necessarily destroy all spores; sterilization is a validated process intended to destroy or remove all microbial life, including spores.

7. What is laboratory quality control?

Quality control is the systematic checking of materials, processes and completed work against defined requirements. Examples include verifying case identification, equipment condition, restoration fit and documented inspection.

8. Differentiate fixed and variable costs.

A fixed cost generally does not change directly with each case in the short term, such as rent. A variable cost changes with production volume, such as case-specific consumables.

9. What is the difference between ethics and jurisprudence?

Ethics concerns professional duties and responsible conduct; jurisprudence concerns laws and legal principles governing practice. Both influence confidentiality, communication and working within one's role.

10. List key stages in laboratory case management.

Receive and identify the case → check prescription → handle and decontaminate appropriately → plan and fabricate → inspect → document, package and dispatch with clear communication.
