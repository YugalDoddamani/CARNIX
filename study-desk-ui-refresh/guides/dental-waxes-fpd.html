The question

Steps involved in fabrication of fixed partial denture (FPD) coping fabrication — which waxes are used, and for what?

01

Why colour codes?

Hardness, elasticity and melting behaviour — matched to each step.

In FPD coping fabrication, various colour-coded waxes serve specific purposes, and choosing the right wax type is essential for accurate FPD coping fabrication. Each colour signals a distinct hardness, elasticity and flow behaviour, so the technician can grab the right stick for modelling, margins, stress areas, trimming, dipping or preformed details without re-melting a general-purpose wax.

Heat & dip→Model the pattern→Form cervical margins→Fill stress-prone areas→Trim & refine→Fit preforms
02

Special wax for the electric wax-heating unit (Vario Therm)

Low melting point, high flow — the first wax on the die.

  • Specially formulated to work with an electrical wax heater that allows precise temperature control and fast heating.
  • Low melting point and a high flow rate, making it easy to apply and manipulate.

Suitable applications during FPD fabrication

Modelling

Creating the wax pattern of the FPD on the master cast.

Trimming

Removing excess wax and refining the shape and contour of the FPD.

Dipping

Coating the wax pattern with a thin layer of wax to increase its strength and thickness.

03

Dark blue wax (hard) and light blue wax (soft)

The everyday modelling pair.

  • Used for modelling of FPD patterns, as they have good carving and sculpting properties.
  • They have different degrees of hardness and elasticity, depending on the application and the technician's preference.
  • The dark blue wax is harder and more rigid; the light blue wax is softer and more flexible.
  • They are also used for trimming excess wax and for dipping for uniform strength.

Dark blue — hard

Rigid, holds form: pattern bodies and areas that must resist distortion while carving.

Light blue — soft

Flexible and forgiving: adapting wax into embrasures and line angles, and building up contours quickly.

04

Lilac wax (medium hard) and red wax (stress-free)

Margins and distortion-prone areas.

Lilac — cervical margins

Medium hard with a fine texture, making it ideal for creating cervical margins — the edges of the FPD that meet the tooth or implant.

Red — stress-free areas

Stress-free with a low shrinkage rate, making it suitable for stress-prone areas — such as connectors and pontics — to prevent distortion during waxing.

Paper tip: pair lilac with “fine texture → cervical margins” and red with “low shrinkage → connectors & pontics” in the same sentence for a complete 2-mark answer.

05

Green wax (hard)

Trimming and shaping without deforming.

  • Used for trimming and shaping the wax pattern, as it has high hardness and stability.
  • It can withstand high temperatures and pressure without deforming or cracking.
  • It also provides a smooth surface and a sharp edge for the wax pattern.
Why a hard wax for trimming?A soft wax drags and rounds off margins when the trimming instrument approaches it. A hard, stable wax keeps its body, so the trimmed surface stays smooth and the edge stays sharp.
06

Yellow wax caps (uniform strength)

The dip step, made consistent.

  • Used for dip application, as they have uniform strength and a consistent thickness.
  • They are pre-formed wax caps that fit over the wax pattern and are heated to adhere to it.
  • They ensure the wax pattern has sufficient thickness and a homogeneous structure for casting.
Heater ready at set temperature→Dip the die with a yellow cap→Cap adheres as a uniform first layer→Coping base of even thickness
07

Wax preforms

Ready-made details that shorten finishing.

  • Used to simplify the wax-up process and shorten the finishing procedure.
  • They are pre-shaped wax pieces that resemble the final shape and size of the FPD.
  • They can be easily adapted to the master cast and require minimal adjustments.

Examples of preforms include ready-made copings, margins and occlusal formers — the class note describes their role generically: fit, adapt, adjust minimally.

Sequence in words

Heat the Vario Therm wax → dip with a yellow cap → model the pattern with the blue waxes → place lilac cervical margins → add red wax in connectors and pontics → trim and shape with green → refine with preforms.

Exam technique

Name the wax, state its property, then its use — three marks per colour. If the question says “steps”, lead with the dipping/modelling sequence and attach the waxes to the steps.
