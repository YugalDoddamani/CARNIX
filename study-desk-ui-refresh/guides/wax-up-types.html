The four types at a glance

  1. Basic diagnostic wax-up — plan the treatment, wax only the deficient areas.
  2. Full-contour wax-up — wax everything to final form; the full-mouth rehabilitation tool.
  3. Case presentation wax-up — show the patient the proposed end result.
  4. Orthodontic wax-up — preview the final outcome of tooth repositioning.

What a wax-up is

A diagnostic wax-up is a wax mock-up built on the study cast to preview form, function and appearance before any clinical or laboratory work is finalised. It is a planning and communication tool — not the final restoration.

It is a study aid, not an official marking scheme. Diagnostic wax-ups are made to a dentist's prescription; case choice, technique and cost vary with the clinician, the case and the laboratory.
01

Why make a wax-up at all?

The wax-up is the cheapest place to find problems.

Definition

A diagnostic wax-up is the planned addition and shaping of wax on a cast of the patient's teeth to preview the intended result of restorative or orthodontic treatment. It lets the dentist and technician see, measure and modify the plan before touching the patient's mouth.

What it gives the team

  1. A visual plan for restorative procedures.
  2. A way to check length, volume, shape and position in wax, not in the mouth.
  3. A communication bridge between dentist, technician and patient.
  4. A base for mock-ups, provisionals and implant-placement determinants where needed.

What it does not do

  1. It does not replace clinical examination or the treatment plan.
  2. It does not guarantee the result — the mouth is not the cast.
  3. It is not one fixed recipe: the type of wax-up must match the purpose.

Generic workflow

Examine the cast & prescription→Choose the wax-up type→Wax to the plan→Dentist reviews & modifies→Use for treatment, mock-up or imprint

Paper tip: in a short answer, define the wax-up, then name the four types before detailing the one asked.

02

The basic diagnostic wax-up

Minimum wax, maximum planning value.

  1. Plans the case: it aids in developing an overall treatment plan for the restorative procedures.
  2. Minimal waxing: wax is placed in the deficient areas only — on the model you can observe that only the deficient areas were built with wax.
  3. Marking the model: models can be marked to depict necessary gingival crown lengthening.
  4. Visualising the correction: wax is then flowed down or up in those areas to provide a good visual of the improvement.

Cusp tips

Cusp tips are added to improve centric stop areas — to eliminate areas that are detrimental to the overall goal of:

  • a proper plane of occlusion,
  • stable centric stops, and
  • maximized anterior guidance function.

When to use a handpiece

  • Incisal length is added to achieve the desired length or position.
  • Lingual contours are added to improve anterior guidance.
Cost and valueOf all the wax-ups, the basic diagnostic wax-up is the least costly in both time and money. It is the best tool for the visualization process.
Mark deficient areas & lengthening lines→Flow wax only into deficient areas→Add cusp tips for stable centric stops→Refine incisal length & lingual contours→Review plan with the dentist
03

The full-contour wax-up

Every tooth waxed to its final form — the rehabilitation tool.

  1. It is used when improved esthetics and contours are included in the treatment plan.
  2. It is similar to the basic wax-up but uses more wax — the whole dentition is built to full contour.
  3. It is used if missing teeth are involved and dental implant placement determinants are necessary.

Key points from the note

  • Teeth are reduced to the point where the technician or laboratory assistant can determine adequate tooth form and function.
  • This is frequently the wax-up of choice for full-mouth rehabilitation situations.
  • Using this mock-up, one can fabricate functionally accurate provisionals for the patient — a direct benefit of the full-contour wax-up.

Before

Worn, tilted or missing teeth; uneven plane of occlusion; deficient contours and unstable centric stops visible on the cast.

After

All teeth built to full contour in wax: restored plane of occlusion, stable stops, guided anterior function and previewed esthetics — ready to index for provisionals.

Reduce teeth on the cast as planned→Wax every tooth to full contour→Verify form, occlusion & implant determinants→Index the mock-up for provisionals
04

The case presentation wax-up

Built to sell the plan to the patient — with pink wax for realism.

  1. Generally requested to show the patient the end result of the personalized treatment plan — it drives patient acceptance.
  2. To create it, the teeth are prepared on the model as they would be in the mouth.
  3. Wax is then built up to proper contour and function.
  4. Often pink baseplate wax is added to the tissue areas to complete the visual effect for the patient.
Know the trade-offs
  • The case presentation wax-up is the least diagnostic of all wax-ups, unless done in tandem with a basic diagnostic wax-up.
  • Provisionals can also be generated from this wax-up — provided the pink wax has not been added to the tissue areas (not common).
  • It requires much more skill and time from the technician or laboratory assistant, which is reflected in the cost.
Prepare teeth on the model→Wax to contour & function→Add pink baseplate wax to tissue→Present to the patient→Gain case acceptance
05

The orthodontic wax-up

Previewing the repositioned smile before the exact phase begins.

On the cast, the irregularly positioned anterior teeth are visible. The wax-up should be done to show the final treatment outcome of the planned repositioning.

  • The dentist will be able to examine the quality of the outcome on the patient's wax-up and make tiny modifications if required.
  • The next phase must be extremely exact — the wax-up imprint.
  • At least two things must be completed to obtain a highly exact imprint: a very well finished wax-up and a very precise impression material.
  • The clinician supplies the dental technician with all information required for a tailored wax-up, based on inspection of the smile and the patient's chief complaint: length, volume, shape, position, etc.
Cast shows irregular anterior teeth→Wax the planned final positions→Dentist examines & fine-tunes→Imprint the finished wax-up exactly→Proceed with the exact next phase

Choosing the right wax-up

Match the type to the purpose, then cost it honestly.

Common confusion

Basic vs case presentation: the basic wax-up plans and diagnoses with minimal wax; the presentation wax-up impresses the patient and usually carries pink tissue wax. Full-contour vs presentation: both wax to full form, but the full-contour wax-up is a functional planning tool for rehabilitation, while the presentation wax-up is a communication tool.

Short-answer skeleton

  1. Define diagnostic wax-up (one sentence).
  2. Name the four types.
  3. For the asked type: purpose, how much wax, one or two key features.
  4. Close with its cost/skill note or one application (provisionals, imprint).
